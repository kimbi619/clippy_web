There are two straightforward ways to read a WhatsApp voice note instead of listening to it. If WhatsApp's own voice message transcripts are available on your phone and in your language, turn them on in WhatsApp's chat settings and transcribe notes inside the app. If they aren't available, or you want to keep the text somewhere else, forward the voice note to an assistant such as Clippy, which replies with the text.

This guide covers both, plus a few habits that make voice notes easier to transcribe in the first place.

## Why read voice notes at all

Voice notes are quick to send and slow to receive. A two-minute note takes two minutes to listen to, can't be skimmed, and is awkward to play in a meeting, on public transport or late at night. It is also hard to find later: you can search your chats for a word someone typed, but not for a word someone said.

Text fixes all of that. You can read it in a few seconds, copy the address or number you need, and come back to it later.

## Option 1: WhatsApp's built-in voice message transcripts

WhatsApp has added voice message transcripts to its apps, but availability depends on your region, your app version and the language of the message. If you can't find the setting described below, your account probably doesn't have the feature yet, or your language isn't supported.

### How to turn it on

The exact wording can change between app versions, but in general:

1. Open WhatsApp and go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **Chats**.
3. Look for **Voice message transcripts** and turn it on.
4. Choose the transcript language if asked.

### How to transcribe a voice note

Once it's on, open a chat with a voice note, then tap and hold the voice note and choose **Transcribe**, or tap the transcript option shown under the message. The text appears under the voice note.

### What to know about built-in transcripts

- **They are generated on your device.** WhatsApp says transcripts are created on your phone, so the audio isn't sent elsewhere for this.
- **Language support is limited.** Only some languages are supported, and messages that mix languages can come out poorly.
- **The text stays in that chat.** It's attached to that voice note on your phone. That suits reading a message quickly, but not keeping a record or collecting notes in one place.

If the built-in feature is available and covers your language, it's the quickest option for reading a single message.

## Option 2: Forward voice notes to Clippy

[Clippy](/blog/introducing-clippy) is an assistant with its own WhatsApp number. Send it a voice note, or forward one someone sent you, and it replies with the text. It's the same chat you use to message Clippy, so your transcripts are all in one place instead of spread across many conversations.

### How to do it

1. [Download the Clippy app](/download) and save Clippy's number to your WhatsApp contacts.
2. In any chat, tap and hold the voice note you want to read.
3. Tap **Forward** and choose Clippy.
4. Clippy replies with the text.

You can also record a voice note straight to Clippy. This is a handy way to dictate a note to yourself: talk while you walk, read it later.

### Seeing your voice notes in the app

The Clippy app keeps your voice notes together. Each one shows a waveform and the transcribed text, so you can skim what was said and still listen to the original when tone matters. The [features page](/features) has more detail.

### When Clippy can't hear clearly

Like any transcription, accuracy depends on the audio. If a voice note is too noisy, too quiet or too broken up to transcribe reliably, Clippy tells you so rather than producing a confident guess. Read the [privacy page](/privacy) for how voice notes you send are handled.

## Which option to use

| If you want to… | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Quickly read one message in a supported language | WhatsApp's built-in transcripts |
| Read notes in a language WhatsApp doesn't support yet | Forward to Clippy |
| Keep all your transcribed notes in one searchable place | Forward to Clippy |
| Dictate notes to yourself | Send voice notes to Clippy |
| Transcribe whole calls, not just messages | [Add Clippy to the call](/blog/how-to-transcribe-whatsapp-calls) |

The two options work fine side by side. Use the built-in one for quick reading and Clippy for anything you want to keep.

## Tips for voice notes that transcribe well

If you send voice notes, a few small habits make them much easier to transcribe — and to listen to.

### Record somewhere quiet

Background noise is the main cause of transcription mistakes. Wind, traffic, a television or a busy café all compete with your voice. Stepping somewhere quieter for thirty seconds helps more than any software setting.

### Hold the phone close

Speak towards the bottom of the phone, where the microphone usually is, at a normal distance. Holding it far away or covering the mic with your hand makes the audio thinner and harder to understand.

### Say the important details clearly

Names, addresses, phone numbers, times and amounts are the parts people most often need from a voice note, and the parts most worth getting right. Slow down for them. For an unusual name or a reference number, spell it out.

### Keep notes focused

One long voice note covering five topics is hard to listen to and hard to read. Several short ones, each about one thing, are easier for everyone. If you do need to cover several points, say how many at the start: "Three things about Saturday."

### Stick to one language per note

Switching languages mid-sentence is natural in many households and workplaces, but it's hard for transcription. If the text matters, try to keep each note in one language.

## A note for people receiving voice notes

If you prefer reading to listening, it's fine to say so. Many people send voice notes because typing on a phone is slow, not because they expect you to listen. With transcription, both people get what suits them: they speak, you read.
